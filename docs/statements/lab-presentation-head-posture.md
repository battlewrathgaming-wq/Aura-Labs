# Lab Presentation Head Posture

Status: Accepted posture statement
Date: 2026-06-01

## Statement

Aura Lab makes refined presentation heads and the tooling needed to explore them.

A presentation head is an offer: a stable, safe-enough, documented presentation window that target projects may adopt through their own authority.

Target projects own what they broadcast through it:

- source meaning
- adapter mapping
- product language
- runtime behavior
- local refinement
- adoption timing

Lab may keep developing newer heads, materials, tools, and presentation features. Those upgrades are optional offers, not forced synchronization.

Lab shelves stable references, learns from target pressure, and returns to exploration.

## Operating Posture

Lab should hand projects a refined window, not a doctrine machine.

The goal is to make the face worth trying while avoiding unearned certainty. Target projects should be free to adapt the head through their own dynamic adapters, then harden only the parts that prove useful under real project pressure.

Accepted deviance is part of the model. Once a project adopts a head, it may bend the presentation to fit local needs. That deviation is feedback unless it breaks meaning, clarity, safety, or the source-project authority boundary.

## What Lab Provides

- stable presentation references
- visual grammar and display primitives
- example input shapes with clear non-contract boundaries
- source/freshness/basis/gap/warning treatments
- safety-floor expectations for display-only heads
- migration or difference notes when a newer head becomes available

## What Lab Does Not Provide

- source-project meaning
- target adapters
- runtime behavior ownership
- bridge or schema authority
- forced upgrades
- shared product doctrine
- downstream maintenance obligations after local adoption

## Handoff Principle

A target project may take a head when it is useful enough to try, not when it is final enough to obey.

The target owns the adapter and the broadcast. Lab owns the reference head and the exploration bench.
